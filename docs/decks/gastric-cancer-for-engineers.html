The stomach, part by part

Anatomy

Cardia

Where the food pipe joins. Cancers here behave more like oesophageal cancer (reflux, obesity).

Fundus, body, antrum ("non-cardia")

Most cases worldwide. Strongly tied to H. pylori. The antrum, near the exit, is a common site.

Pylorus

The exit valve. A tumour here can block it: vomiting, feeling full after a few bites.

Engineer's view: two failure modes by location, with different causes, so they leave different signals in the data.

The stomach wall has layers. How deep the tumour goes is the "T" in staging

Depth = stage

Early gastric cancer

Still in the inner two layers (T1). Often removed through the endoscope, with no open surgery.

Stage = T + N + M

Depth (T), lymph nodes involved (N), spread to far organs (M). Combined into stage I to IV.

How it is seen

Endoscopy and biopsy for the lining, CT scan for nodes and spread, endoscopic ultrasound for depth.

The Correa cascade: a slow pipeline of tissue states

How the common (intestinal) type develops

The lining degrades through known states. Each one can be seen on a biopsy, like reading a system's logs.

  1. Normal lining

    Healthy glands, normal acid.

  2. Chronic gastritis

    Long-running inflammation, usually from H. pylori.

  3. Atrophy

    Glands are lost; the stomach makes less acid.

  4. Intestinal metaplasia

    Stomach cells replaced by intestine-like cells.

  5. Dysplasia

    Abnormal cells: the last step before cancer.

  6. Cancer

    Invades the wall (adenocarcinoma).

H. pylori: a common bacterium behind most cases

14 days

antibiotics + acid blocker; re-test 4+ weeks later

Treatment prevents cancer

Trials of clearing the infection show roughly a third to a half fewer gastric cancers later. The earlier in the cascade, the bigger the benefit.

The "African enigma"

Infection is very common in Africa, yet reported cancer rates looked lower than expected. Part of the gap is likely missed diagnoses: few endoscopies, few pathology labs.

Sources: IARC Monographs vol. 61 (1994) · Plummer et al., Int J Cancer 2015 · Ford et al., Cochrane review of eradication trials · Hooi et al., Gastroenterology 2017 · Maastricht VI/Florence consensus 2022

How much each factor multiplies the risk

Risk factors

"Relative risk 3" means three times the yearly chance compared with someone without that factor. Factors multiply: an older man with H. pylori who smokes carries many times the baseline risk.

Age outweighs them all: risk climbs steeply after 50.

Known, not yet in our model

  • Epstein–Barr virus (about 1 in 10 tumours)
  • Inherited CDH1 gene change (hereditary diffuse type)
  • Pernicious anaemia, past stomach surgery
  • Obesity and reflux (cardia tumours)

Two main types, with different stories

Under the microscope (Lauren classification)

Intestinal type

Cells still form gland-like rings.

  • Older people, more often men
  • Follows the Correa cascade; driven by H. pylori, salt, smoking
  • Falling in countries where H. pylori is falling

Diffuse type

Single scattered cells ("signet ring").

  • Younger people; women as often as men
  • Less tied to the cascade; some cases inherited (CDH1)
  • Spreads under the lining, hard to see; can stiffen the whole stomach

Why it matters for us: young-onset cases are rising in several countries and are more often diffuse. A model that learns from older men's patterns can miss them.

Source: Laurén P. Acta Pathol Microbiol Scand 1965 · young-onset trends: GBD 2019 analysis, Gastric Cancer 2024

Symptoms arrive late, and early ones look like indigestion

Why it is found late

Years

Silent

No symptoms; only a biopsy shows it.

Months

Vague

Pain, bloating, early fullness.

Weeks–months

Alarm

Something structural is wrong.

Acid medicine masks it. PPIs calm the pain for weeks, so everyone feels reassured.

Anaemia gets another label. Slow bleeding lowers Hb; in malaria areas it is often treated as malaria or worms first.

Each visit looks normal alone. The pattern across visits is what is abnormal.

Alarm features

Endoscopy
  • Weight loss without trying
  • Trouble swallowing
  • Repeated vomiting
  • Bleeding or iron-deficiency anaemia
  • A lump in the upper belly

Stage decides the treatment

Treatment

Stage I

Curable

Where

Lining and inner wall

Treatment

Removal through the endoscope, or surgery. Most patients are cured.

Stage II

Good chance

Where

Deeper wall, or a few lymph nodes

Treatment

Removing part or all of the stomach (gastrectomy) plus chemotherapy before and after.

Stage III

Harder

Where

Through the wall, many nodes

Treatment

Same as stage II, with a lower chance of cure.

Stage IV

Not curable

Where

Liver, lungs, abdominal lining

Treatment

Chemotherapy, sometimes targeted (HER2) or immune therapy; symptom care, nutrition.

For Rwanda: most patients still arrive at stage III–IV, where options are limited and costly. Every stage we move left saves lives and money.

How other countries catch it early, and what fits Rwanda

Prevention and early detection

Prevent

Stop the cascade

  • H. pylori "test and treat"
  • Less salt and smoked food; refrigeration
  • No smoking
Screen everyone

Japan and South Korea

Regular endoscopy for adults from 40–50 years. Most cancers are now found early. It needs many endoscopists and machines.

Find the few

Risk-based triage

Scopes are scarce, so we cannot scope everyone. Use the records to find the small group most likely to need one, and send them first.

Words you will hear tomorrow

Cheat sheet

Dyspepsia
Indigestion: upper-belly pain, bloating, early fullness.
PPI
Strong acid blocker, e.g. omeprazole.
Endoscopy (OGD)
Camera through the mouth into the stomach; takes biopsies.
Biopsy / histology
Tissue read under a microscope. The only proof of cancer.
Adenocarcinoma
Cancer of gland cells: about 9 in 10 stomach cancers.
Intestinal metaplasia
Lining replaced by intestine-like cells; pre-cancerous.
OLGA / OLGIM
Biopsy scores of how severe atrophy or metaplasia is.
TNM
Tumour depth, Nodes, Metastasis: becomes stage I–IV.
Gastrectomy, D2
Removing part or all of the stomach, with its lymph nodes.
FLOT
Four-drug chemotherapy given before and after surgery.
Hb
Haemoglobin. Falls slowly when a tumour bleeds.
ECOG
0–4 fitness score; decides if a patient can take treatment.

Six questions to ask the oncologist

Bring to the meeting
